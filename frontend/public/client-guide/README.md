# Your case records — a short guide

Your attorney's office has given you access to the financial records in your
case. This page explains what you can see and what you can do.

**You cannot change any record here.** Nothing you do alters a statement, a
figure, or a document. The office reviews everything you mark.

---

## What you can see

| Page | What is on it |
| ---- | ------------- |
| **Accounts** | Every account in your case, and what the office holds for each one |
| **Statements held** | Which months are on file, and which months are still missing |
| **Transactions** | Every line from every statement, with a search |

**You will see the other side's accounts too**, where their statements have been
produced in your case. That is intended.

---

## Marking a transaction

If you see something your attorney should look at, mark it.

1. Open the **Transactions** page.
2. Find the transaction.
3. Choose a tag from the list.

**What happens then:**

- The office sees what you marked, in a list of its own.
- Nothing in the record changes. **A tag is a note.**
- The tags you can choose from are set by the office. You cannot add your own.

**Mark anything you are not sure about.** A short note from you is far cheaper
than a transaction nobody asked about.

---

## A tag with a line through it

The office reviewed that tag and removed it. **You cannot put it back.**

That is not a judgement about you. It usually means the transaction turned out
to be something else, or it is covered elsewhere. **If you disagree, tell your
attorney's office** — they can restore it.

---

## "Tagging is paused"

The office has turned marking off for now. This is normal before a hearing: the
set of marked transactions has to stop changing before anybody argues from it.

**Nothing you marked is lost.** Reading still works as usual.

---

## Searching by amount

Two boxes: **Amount at least** and **Amount at most**. Type the figure without a
sign to find money **in and out**.

| You type | You get |
| -------- | ------- |
| `5000` | Every transaction of $5,000 or more, in **either** direction |
| `-5000` | Money **out** only, $5,000 or more |
| `+5000` | Money **in** only, $5,000 or more |

**An example of why that matters.** You remember $5,000 leaving the joint
account. Type `5000` with no sign, and you get the money that left *and* the
$5,000 that arrived somewhere else — which is usually the thing you are
actually arguing about.

Type the smaller figure in **Amount at least**. The search refuses a range that
mixes `-` and `+`; leave both signs off instead.

---

## Two things worth knowing about the figures

**A credit-card purchase shows as a positive number.** Amounts are signed the
way the statement prints them, and a purchase *increases* what you owe. A
payment to the card shows as negative.

**Some transactions have no date.** A few kinds of statement — insurance
policies, for example — print totals for a period rather than dated lines.
Those lines will not appear in a search by date.

---

## If something looks wrong

**Tell your attorney's office.** Do not assume they already know.

The records here were read from the statements by software, and the office
checks them — but you know your own accounts far better than any reader does.
A missing month, a figure that looks wrong, an account you do not recognise:
all of it is worth saying.
